Quizata

Why Quizata exists

Most of us carry a rough mental model of the world — how rich, how healthy, how fast-changing it is — built from headlines, not data. Those headlines skew toward what's alarming or novel, so the model drifts. Quizata is a two-minute daily check against reality: five real numbers, one slider each, no multiple choice to hide behind.

It's the same idea behind Hans Rosling's "Ignorance Test," turned into a daily habit instead of a one-time quiz.

Sourcing standard

Every statistic links to a real, reputable, primary source — the World Bank, UN agencies, IEA, Our World in Data, Pew Research, national statistical offices, and similar — along with the year the figure is "as of." Nothing is generated, estimated, or paraphrased from a secondary article. If we can't verify a number against its original source, it doesn't ship.

We also try to avoid stats chosen purely to shock — the goal is a more accurate picture of the world, not a scarier or rosier one.

Scoring

Each guess starts with an accuracy score, 0–100, based on how close it is to the real answer relative to the slider's range (on a logarithmic scale for numbers that span orders of magnitude, like populations or dollar amounts, so "off by 2x" always costs about the same regardless of size).

Half your final score for a question is that accuracy, and half is how you compare to everyone else who answered it that day — so the game is measuring your sense of the world both against reality and against the crowd's. Early in a question's life, before enough people have answered, you're scored on accuracy alone. Your daily total is out of 500.

Power-ups

Once a day you can narrow a question's slider range to a third of its width, centered wherever you've currently got the slider — useful if your instinct is roughly right and you want finer control, useless (or worse) if it isn't. Once a day you can also take a second guess on any question, with the better of the two counting. Using either caps that question's maximum possible score, so they're a real tradeoff, not a free upgrade.

Your data

Your streak and history live only in your browser. What gets shared is minimal and anonymous: a running count and score total per question per day, used only to compute the crowd comparison above — never a per-player record of what you guessed.
